Lesson 6 · Going deeper · Align and Advance

Introduce the solution, then move the sale

Align is the first moment the product enters the call, and it should feel like an answer to what the customer just told you. Advance turns that answer into a dated commitment, and the work after the yes turns a first case into a habit.

Claims come from approved materials. The property, any number, rate or comparison, and the boundary all come from the IFU and approved product materials. If you cannot say which property answers which pain, Diagnose was not finished.

Why only one product

The customer described one problem. Three products tells them you were waiting for your turn to talk. You can bring in a second product later, against a second problem they describe.

Why the boundary helps you

Surgeons trust reps who know where a product stops working. The boundary also keeps the conversation on label. For FLOSEAL, the IFU gives you several: do not inject or compress it into blood vessels; it swells about 10 to 20 percent after application; remove excess by gentle irrigation; it is not for prophylactic use.

Stop selling when they've already bought

Buying signals sound like logistics, not enthusiasm. When you hear one, stop presenting. Confirm the step.

Buying signals
"Isn't it already on our shelf?"
"Who would show my scrub team?"
"Can we have it Thursday?"
"Talk to Kim about the card."
Selling past the yes
"Great question. Before that, let me walk you through the rest of the data."
This reopens a decision the customer had already made, and spends time they don't have.

Advance, not continuation

Neil Rackham's research on large sales separates an advance, where the customer commits to something that moves the sale forward, from a continuation, where the conversation simply goes on. A dated no beats an undated maybe.

ContinuationAdvance
"Keep in touch.""Bring it for Thursday's two fusions. I'll be in the room."
"Send me the literature.""Kim will add it to my card this week."
"Check back next quarter.""Let's look at my end times together on the 30th."

After the yes: Follow Up, Pull Through, Continue

A first case is not adoption. These three happen in order.

1. Follow Up

Do what you said, when you said.

  • Confirm the first case date and room.
  • Send whatever you promised, the same day.
  • Be where you said you'd be.

2. Pull Through

Make the new way the default way.

  • Product on the shelf in the right OR.
  • On the surgeon's preference card.
  • Scrub team trained on prep.
  • First cases covered.
  • Review usage and the customer's number.

3. Continue the sale

Grow from the win.

  • Next case type for the same surgeon.
  • Next surgeon in their network, from AcuityMD.
  • Next department or specialty.
  • Every new customer starts at Connect.
