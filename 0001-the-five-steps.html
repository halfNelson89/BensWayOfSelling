Lesson 1 · The framework

Five steps, one job each

A sales call is a conversation the customer should want to keep having. The Baxter Way of Selling gives it five steps in a fixed order. Each step has one job. Skip a job and the next step has nothing to stand on.

01
Connect
Earn the conversation.
02
Discover
Get them talking about their world.
03
Diagnose
Turn the story into their number.
04
Align
One product answers their pain.
05
Advance
A dated, mutual next step.

The rule that holds it together

Nothing before Align mentions a product.

You cannot quantify a problem nobody has described, and you cannot show fit without a number in the customer's own words. Gold marks Align throughout this course because it is the only place the product enters.

The steps

01

Connect

Job: earn the conversation. Open with a sourced, national parallel fact and an open question about the customer's institution. Nothing about us.

A great Connect is decided before you walk in. Pre-call planning is now part of this step: you choose the fact, the question, and the next step you want before the call starts. Lesson 3 covers it in depth.

Sounds like
"The American Red Cross declared an emergency blood shortage in January 2024. How did that show up in your hospital's planning?"
Not this
"Hi Doctor, do you have a minute to hear about FLOSEAL?"
Product first. The surgeon decides in one sentence whether the call is worth having.

Done whenThey are talking about their world, not about you.

02

Discover

Job: get the customer talking until they carry 70 percent of the conversation. Open, neutral questions. Follow the answer, not your script.

Shape the conversation. Do not introduce a problem. When you name a problem for the customer, they defend their current way of working. When you ask how the work goes, the challenge surfaces on its own.

New in this version: four tools from Chris Voss

  • Mirror. Repeat their last one to three words with a rising tone, then stay silent.
  • Label. Name what you observe. Start with "It seems" or "It sounds like," never "I think" or "What I'm hearing."
  • Accusation audit. Say the negative thing they are already thinking before they say it.
  • "That's right" checkpoint. Before you move to Align, summarize what you heard and listen for the words. "That's right" means they feel understood. "You're right" is a polite brush-off.
Sounds like
"Tell me about where the time goes in a typical two-level fusion."
Not this
"Wouldn't you say bleeding is a big problem in your fusions?"
Problem framing plus a leading question. It announces the answer and dares them to disagree.

Done whenYou can describe how their work runs today in their words, and they have said "That's right" to your summary.

03

Diagnose

Job: turn the story into the customer's number. How often, how long or how much, what happens next, and who feels it. In their units, not yours.

A number the customer builds is a fact they own. A number you hand them is a claim they can argue with. When the number lands, stop talking and let it sit.

Sounds like
"How many of your Thursday fusions get into that pack-and-wait cycle, and what does it do to your last case?"
Not this
"Our data shows you're probably losing forty minutes a day."
Your number, not theirs. They will debate the math instead of owning the problem.

Done whenThere is a number in their words and a name attached to who feels it.

04

Align

Job: answer the pain the customer described with one product, one insight about that product that ties directly to the pain, and one honest boundary.

Align starts with "Based on what you told me" and then uses their words. The claim and any number come from approved materials. The boundary is what makes the claim believable.

Sounds like
"Based on what you told me, the time goes into packing and re-checking. FLOSEAL flows into an irregular bleeding bed instead of sitting on top of it. It swells after it goes in, so near the dura you'd use the least that works and irrigate the excess."
Not this
"We have FLOSEAL, TISSEEL and PERCLOT, so whatever you need, we've got it."
A portfolio dump. Three products tells the surgeon you did not hear the one problem.

Done whenThey react to the fit and start asking how it would work in their room.

05

Advance

Job: secure a concrete, mutual, time-bound next step. A dated no beats an undated maybe.

Two additions in this version. First, stop selling when they have already bought. When the customer asks how to get it, confirm the step and stop presenting. Second, the sale does not end at yes: Follow Up, then Pull Through, then Continue the sale.

Sounds like
"Can we open it for your Thursday fusions? I'll run prep with your scrub team Wednesday at 2 and stand in the room Thursday."
Not this
"Great, I'll check back with you sometime."
An undated maybe. Nothing moves and nobody commits.

Done whenThere is a date, an owner, and something both of you will do.

Practice: name the step

Each card is one line from a real-sounding call. Pick the step it belongs to. The order shuffles every time you run it, and missed cards come back for a retry.

Primary source

Read Never Split the Difference by Chris Voss and Tahl Raz (Harper Business, 2016), chapters 2 and 3, on mirroring and labeling. They are the source of the new Discover tools and the fastest way to hear them done well.
