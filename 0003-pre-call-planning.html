Lesson 3 · Going deeper · Connect

Pre-call planning wins the Connect

The first thirty seconds of a call are decided at your desk the night before. A pre-call plan gives you a fact worth opening with, a question that gets the customer talking, and a next step you already know you want.

Why Connect lives or dies in planning

A surgeon gives a rep a few seconds to prove the conversation is worth having. Small talk spends those seconds on nothing. A product pitch spends them on you. A parallel fact spends them on the customer's world, and it does it in a way they never have to defend.

That is why pre-call planning is now part of Connect. You cannot improvise a sourced fact, and you cannot improvise the question that turns it into a conversation.

The pre-call plan, line by line

Seven lines. Fill every one before the call. The worked example is a cardiac surgeon at a fictional hospital.

LineWhat goes hereWorked example: Dr. Sam Okafor, cardiac surgery
1. Who, and what they're measured onRole, case mix and volume from AcuityMD, network, and the metrics their leadership watches.CABG and valve, about six cases a week. Program reports STS outcomes. Works closely with perfusion and anesthesia.
2. Their reality, as a hypothesisHow you think the work runs today. Written as something to test, not something to sell to.He may lose time at closure to diffuse oozing after long pump runs. I will ask where the end of the case goes; I will not tell him.
3. The parallel factNational, sourced, about the institution, with a clear line to our solution. It must pass all five tests below.A 2025 joint consensus statement from ERAS Cardiac and the Society for the Advancement of Patient Blood Management recommends tracking bleeding quality metrics beyond reexploration rate.
4. The opening questionOpen, about the institution, impossible to answer with yes or no."How is your program thinking about bleeding metrics beyond reexploration?"
5. The Discover question that followsWrite it before you write the Connect line. If you can't, pick another fact."Where in a case does bleeding decide how the rest of your day goes?"
6. Accusation audit listWhat they are probably already thinking about reps, new products, and this call.Another rep with a product for the rare case. Gone after the in-service. Costs more than what we use now.
7. Objective and the Advance you'll ask forSpecific, dated, mutual. What has to be true at the end of this call.Agree on one bleeding metric he cares about, and book 15 minutes with him and the blood management lead before month end.

Five tests for a parallel fact

TestQuestion to ask yourself
SourcedCould I show the customer where it came from?
NationalIs it about the field, so the customer never has to defend their own results?
ParallelDoes it sit beside hemostasis (cost, time, blood supply, quality metrics) rather than being a product claim?
Line to the solutionIs there a path from this fact, through Discover and Diagnose, to a pain one of our products answers?
Opens DiscoverCan I write the Discover question that follows it? If not, the fact is a door to nowhere.

Hypothesis, not assumption

Chris Voss teaches negotiators to walk in with hypotheses they will test, so the conversation can surprise them. An assumption tells you what to pitch. A hypothesis tells you what to ask. Line 2 of the plan is always a hypothesis.

What to pull from AcuityMD

  • Case mix and volume by procedure, so your hypothesis fits their real week.
  • Sites and block days, so your Advance can name a real date.
  • The surgeon's network: partners, referrers and the team around them. These become your owners in Diagnose and your next Connect after the sale.
  • Your Cohort label on the surgeon, so the plan lives where you plan your day.
