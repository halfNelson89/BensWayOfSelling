Lesson 5 · Going deeper · Discover to Diagnose

Listen for it, size it, agree on it

The customer tells you where the challenge is long before they call it one. This lesson covers what to listen for, how to turn it into their number, and how to agree on it with the status quo before you say a word about product.

Six signals to listen for

Each signal tells you which tool to use next.

SignalSounds likeYour next move
A workaround"We just pack it and wait." "Kim keeps the sponges coming."Mirror it. Workarounds are where the status quo hides its cost.
Frequency words"Every Thursday." "Again." "Some days."Diagnose: how often, in a typical week or month?
Time and count words"Three rounds." "Fifteen minutes." "My last case."Diagnose: what happens next when that happens?
Emotion"Honestly..." "It drives me crazy." A sigh.Label it: "It sounds like that's the part of the day you dread."
Other people's names"Paul sends the report." "Kim runs the team."Diagnose the owner: who else feels it? These are your influencers.
Status quo defense"It's worked for twenty years." "It's fine most days."Don't argue. Ask what it costs on the days it isn't fine.

Your real competitor is the status quo

Most customers are not choosing between you and another company. They are choosing between you and changing nothing. Their current way works well enough that they chose it. Your job is not to attack it. Your job is to help them see what it costs, in their own numbers.

Diagnose: four questions, in their units

Diagnose turns a story into a number the customer owns. Work down the ladder. Use their words for time, cases and people. Here it is with a gynecologic oncologist at a fictional hospital.

How often
"How many of your robotic cases end with a second look at the cuff before you close?"
Dr. Shah
"Maybe one in three."
How long or how much
"How much time does that second look add?"
Dr. Shah
"Ten minutes. Twenty if I have to re-dock."
What happens next
"What happens to your afternoon when that happens twice?"
Dr. Shah
"My 2 p.m. starts at 3, and anesthesia is not happy with me."
Who feels it
"Who hears about that besides you?"
Dr. Shah
"The charge nurse, then the OR director at the monthly review."

Then stop talking. Let the number land. The customer just did the math out loud for the first time. Silence lets it register as their problem, not your pitch.

Agree on the revealed challenge

Before Align, check that you and the customer see the same thing. Summarize in four parts, in their words, then ask.

The "that's right" checkpoint

Situation + their number + the consequence + who feels it. "Did I get that right?"

"So about one in three robotic cases needs a second look at the cuff, that's ten to twenty minutes, and on a two-case afternoon your 2 p.m. slides to 3 and the OR director hears about it. Did I get that right?"

"That's right."
She feels understood. The challenge is hers, sized in her terms. Move to Align.
"You're right." "Sure." "I guess."
A polite brush-off. Something in your summary is yours, not hers. Go back and ask.

If she agrees with the story but can't size it ("it's annoying, not sure it costs much"), go back to Diagnose, not Discover.
